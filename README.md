**Project Title**: Analyzing Factors Associated with Traffic Crash Severity in California Using 2025 CCRS Data

## Dataset Information
**Dataset**: California Crash Reporting System (CCRS), Crashes_2025

**Source**: California Open Data Portal (data.ca.gov / publish.data.ca.gov)

**Direct link**: https://publish.data.ca.gov/dataset/ccrs/resource/9f4fc839-122d-4595-a146-43bc4ed16f46

**Format & Size**: CSV, approximately 186 MB, 402,460 records

Why this dataset: Official government data, a large dataset, and a real-world traffic safety problem.
Abstract 

This project will analyze 2025 traffic crash data from the California Crash Reporting System (CCRS) to identify patterns and factors associated with crash frequency and severity. The dataset contains approximately 402,460 reported collision records from across California, with variables including date and time, location, weather and road conditions, lighting, collision type, and the number of people injured or killed. The primary research question is whether environmental factors, such as weather and lighting, and temporal factors, such as time of day and day of the week, are significantly associated with crash severity.

The analysis will follow an iterative data science pipeline. After acquiring the large CSV file, we will conduct exploratory analysis and create visualizations to understand distributions and identify issues. We will then clean the data and engineer features. Selected features will be rescaled as needed before the data is split into training and test sets. We will build and evaluate statistical or machine learning models to quantify relationships between environmental and temporal factors and crash severity, revisiting earlier stages as needed to refine the data or approach. Finally, we will interpret and present the results in the context of real-world traffic safety, highlighting conditions that may call for greater awareness or targeted interventions. This project is original in its use of recent, official state-level data rather than commonly used tutorial datasets. It is also personally and practically relevant because traffic safety is a major public health issue. Understanding which conditions are associated with more severe crashes can help identify ways to reduce injuries and fatalities through greater awareness, infrastructure decisions, or targeted interventions.
